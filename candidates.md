# Candidate lines — A Day of One-Line Pitches

Chosen line: **#7** (185 characters).

Judge's criteria, in order (RULES.txt): (1) makes the case to a friend who has
never coded, in one read; (2) persuasion — a concrete reason to say yes now,
handing over the idea feels easy and low risk; (3) clarity — one read, plain
words, no jargon; (4) truth — no overpromising; (5) warmth and craft — sounds
like a person talking to a friend, a fresh turn of phrase, not a template.

## Candidates (all one sentence, all ≤ 240 characters)

### 1. — 174 chars
> Tell an AI agent on make.fun what your app should do, in your own words, and by tonight you could be tapping a real first version of the idea you've only ever talked about.

1. Case: direct address, names agent + place + the friend's idea. Strong.
2. Persuasion: "by tonight you could be tapping a real first version" is
   concrete and immediate; "could" keeps it low-pressure. Strong.
3. Clarity: plain words, one read. Strong.
4. Truth: "could be" hedges the timing, no hard promise. Strong.
5. Warmth: "the idea you've only ever talked about" is relatable, but the
   line leans on the time promise more than on a human turn. Good, not great.

### 2. — 180 chars
> Describe the app you've always wanted in plain words, and an AI agent on make.fun will build a real first version for you to try — the hardest part is finally saying it out loud.

1. Case: speaks to the friend's long-held idea. Strong.
2. Persuasion: "real first version for you to try" is concrete; "the hardest
   part is saying it out loud" makes handing the idea over feel trivially
   easy. Strong.
3. Clarity: plain. Strong.
4. Truth: "will build a real first version" is the platform's premise. Good.
5. Warmth: the closing turn is fresh and human, like encouragement from a
   friend. Strong.

### 3. — 157 chars
> You bring the idea in plain words, an AI agent on make.fun does all the coding, and you get a working first version to try before you decide anything at all.

1. Case: clear division of labour. Good.
2. Persuasion: "before you decide anything at all" is low risk. Good.
3. Clarity: plain. Strong.
4. Truth: holds up. Strong.
5. Warmth: procedural, no human turn of phrase; reads like instructions.
   Weak.

### 4. — 174 chars
> Let an AI agent on make.fun build your first app from a plain-words description — you describe it, it codes it, and you try the working version before committing to anything.

1. Case: "Let an AI agent build your first app" states the ask. Good.
2. Persuasion: "before committing to anything" is low risk, but no reason to
   say yes *now*. Middling.
3. Clarity: plain, though "you describe it, it codes it" is choppy. Good.
4. Truth: holds up. Strong.
5. Warmth: no fresh phrase; the middle clause reads like a manual. Weak.

### 5. — 161 chars
> Your app idea doesn't need code from you, just words: tell an AI agent on make.fun what it should do and it will build you a first version you can actually try.

1. Case: "just words" names the friend's only job. Strong.
2. Persuasion: "a first version you can actually try" is concrete. Good.
3. Clarity: the colon makes it two beats; still one read. Good.
4. Truth: holds up. Strong.
5. Warmth: punchy opening, but the rest is flat. Middling.

### 6. — 170 chars
> Give your app idea to an AI agent on make.fun in plain words and it will hand you back a working first version to play with — you don't need to code, only to imagine it.

1. Case: names the handover directly. Good.
2. Persuasion: "to play with" is low stakes and easy. Strong.
3. Clarity: plain. Strong.
4. Truth: holds up. Strong.
5. Warmth: "hand you back" and "only to imagine it" are warm turns; "Give
   your app idea to" slightly overstates the handover. Good.

### 7. CHOSEN — 185 chars
> Tell an AI agent on make.fun about the app you've always wanted, in plain words, and it will build you a working first version to try — the only hard part is finally saying it out loud.

1. Case: opens by naming the agent, the place, and the friend's long-held
   idea in one breath — the ask lands immediately. Strong.
2. Persuasion: a working first version to try is the concrete payoff, and
   "the only hard part is saying it out loud" makes handing the idea over
   feel like the easiest step, not a risk. Strong.
3. Clarity: plain words throughout, one read, no jargon. Strong.
4. Truth: "will build you a working first version" is what the platform
   does; nothing about price, speed, or success is promised. Strong.
5. Warmth: the closing turn is a fresh, human encouragement — a friend
   talking to a friend, not a slogan. Strong.

### 8. — 168 chars
> Just describe your app idea in plain words and an AI agent on make.fun will build you a working first version to try — no coding, no jargon, just your idea turned real.

1. Case: direct. Good.
2. Persuasion: concrete payoff, but no reason to say yes now. Good.
3. Clarity: plain. Strong.
4. Truth: holds up. Strong.
5. Warmth: "no coding, no jargon" is a slogan-ish list; "just your idea
   turned real" is slightly awkward. Middling.

## Why #7 wins

#7 keeps #2's best line (the closing turn) and tightens the opening: it names
the agent and the friend's long-held idea up front, and "working first
version" is more concrete than "real first version". It beats #1 on craft
(criterion 5) and matches it on persuasion, with a cleaner low-risk frame
than #1's hedged "by tonight you could be". It beats #6 because the handover
is framed as talking, not giving something away. #3, #4, #5 and #8 are clear
and true but read like instructions or slogans, with no human turn of phrase.
